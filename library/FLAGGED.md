# Questions to check

While writing the misconception notes, these questions looked wrong, ambiguous or beyond the syllabus. The game still uses them as keyed.

- **Ch 4 · Exploring Diversity of Matter using Separation Techniques, c04q53**: Groundwater flows through sand and rocks in a siltation pond. This is  
  A siltation pond mainly works by letting particles settle out, so the stem mixes settling with filtration. The key still fits the sand and rocks.
- **Ch 9 · Application of Forces and Transfer of Energy, c09q11**: A Maglev train floats above its track because of  
  Some real Maglev systems, such as the Shanghai Maglev, lift the train by attraction between magnets, so option A could be argued. The key fits the textbook model.
- **Ch 9 · Application of Forces and Transfer of Energy, c09q59**: A car has 100 J of total energy at the top. What is its total energy at the bottom?  
  The key assumes no energy is lost to friction or air resistance, or that heat produced still counts in the car's total. The stem does not say which.
- **Ch 10 · Transfer of Heat Energy and its Effects, c10q14**: When the air in a hot air balloon is heated, its mass  
  A real hot air balloon is open at the bottom, so heated air expands out and the mass of air inside decreases. The key assumes a fixed amount of air.
- **Ch 10 · Transfer of Heat Energy and its Effects, c10q28**: The bimetallic coil in a food thermometer unwinds. What has happened to the temperature?  
  Whether a heated bimetallic coil unwinds or winds tighter depends on which metal is on the inside, so the stem relies on the textbook's design.
- **Ch 12 · Interactions within Ecosystems, c12q2**: Why is fresh water a limited resource?  
  About 2.5% of Earth's water is fresh, so the keyed option is only correct if it means fresh water that is easily available.
- **Ch 13 · Electrical Systems, c13q35**: A second resistor is added in parallel. The bulb will become  
  The answer depends on where the resistor is added, so the stem should say it is in parallel with a resistor that is in series with the bulb.
- **Ch 14 · Human Digestive System, c14q3**: An amoeba has no digestive system. How does it get nutrients?  
  An amoeba actually engulfs food particles and digests them inside food vacuoles, so 'absorbs them from its surroundings' is a simplification.
- **Ch 14 · Human Digestive System, c14q13**: Which organ carries out chemical digestion but no physical digestion?  
  The small intestine also mixes food by muscle contractions, so whether it does no physical digestion depends on how the textbook defines it.
- **Ch 14 · Human Digestive System, c14q27**: Besides starting starch digestion, saliva  
  Saliva does contain substances that kill some bacteria, so option A is not strictly false.
- **Ch 14 · Human Digestive System, c14q42**: Cells use simple sugars to  
  Extra simple sugars can be turned into fat in the body, so option D is not strictly false.
- **Ch 16 · Human Sexual Reproductive System, c16q60**: Which is a long-term effect of gonorrhoea?  
  Some textbooks list blindness (option B) as an effect of gonorrhoea in babies born to infected mothers, so B could be argued as correct.
- **Built-in questions, b10**  
  Option C 'A part of a tissue' is also true of many cells, so a student who picks it is marked wrong for a true statement.
- **Built-in questions, b45**  
  A limit on the number of cell divisions (the Hayflick limit) is beyond the Lower Secondary Science syllabus, and some cells such as stem cells keep dividing.
